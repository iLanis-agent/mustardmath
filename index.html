Mustard math

Homemade mustard is a soak, a grind and a jar - and every recipe hedges the one number that matters: how much liquid the seeds actually need. Mustard is seeds plus liquid, and the liquid is a multiple of the seeds.

Weigh the seeds

The problem it solves

Soak recipes say "cover the seeds," which works until the batch doubles and the jar count triples. Mustard math turns seed weight into milliliters: the soak at your chosen consistency, the vinegar-water split that decides whether it bites or soothes, and the whole batch sized backwards from how many jars you want to fill.

What it does not do

The arithmetic is exact; the norms are not. Seed variety, grind and heat all change the result, and the bands here are flagged craft conventions, not measurements of your ingredients. It sizes the batch - you still taste the mustard.
